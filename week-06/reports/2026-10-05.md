## 2. Findings

1. **new-arrivals: click-through rate halved.** Impressions rose (30,000 → 32,000) and spend
   rose ($540 → $560), but clicks fell from 900 to 480. Purchases fell from 18 to 8, so cost
   per purchase went from $30 to $70 and ROAS from 2.33 to 1.00. Revenue per purchase stayed
   at $70, so the drop is in clicks and purchases, not order value.
   - Did the ad creative, headline or landing link change in the week of 5 October?
   - Did targeting or placements change so the ads reached a less interested audience?
   - Was click tracking working all week (for example, a broken tracking link or tag)?

2. **retargeting: conversion rate and order value both rose.** Clicks rose slightly
   (480 → 500) at the same spend ($600), purchases rose from 20 to 24, and revenue per
   purchase rose from $90 to $100. Cost per purchase fell from $30 to $25 and ROAS rose
   from 3.00 to 4.00.
   - Did a promotion, price change or new product make retargeted visitors buy more, or
     buy higher-value items?
   - Did the retargeting audience change (for example, more recent site visitors)?

3. **Purchases may have moved between new-arrivals and retargeting.** new-arrivals lost
   10 attributed purchases while retargeting gained 4; the other two campaigns barely moved.
   - Are people who first saw a new-arrivals ad now buying after a retargeting ad, so the
     purchase is credited to retargeting?
   - Did the attribution settings (window or model) change between the two weeks?
